Aurélien Dunand-Pallaz Sets Men’s Supported FKT on Corsica’s GR20 Trail

France’s Aurélien Dunand-Pallaz set a new men’s supported FKT on Corsica’s GR20 trail.
By Eszter Horanyi on September 25, 2026 | Comments
Support us! iRunFar may earn a small commission from affiliate links in this article. Learn more.

When Aurélien Dunand-Pallaz arrived at Conca, on the French island of Corsica, at 8:29 a.m. on Friday, September 25, 2026, having completed the 166-kilometer (103 miles) GR20 route in 27 hours and 29 minutes, he reset a fastest known time that had stood for less than two months.

Completing one of the most technical routes on the island, the Frenchman broke fellow French athlete François D’Haene’s previous fastest known time (FKT) of 29 hours, 46 minutes, and 20 seconds by a massive 2 hours, 17 minutes, and 20 seconds. It was the second FKT that D’Haene lost this week, as Joe “Stringbean” McConaughy also set the men’s supported John Muir Trail FKT, which D’Haene formerly held.

Aurélien Dunand-Pallaz after setting a new FKT on the GR20. Photo: @rising.story

Dunand-Pallaz started from Calenzana at 5 a.m. on Thursday, September 24. The GR20 route has approximately 12,000 meters (39,400 feet) of elevation gain as it crosses the Mediterranean island from the northwest to the southeast. The route is considered one of the more demanding long-distance trails in Europe, as it requires plenty of scrambling and technical descending over rocky, mountainous terrain.

The men’s supported FKT on the route has been firmly in French hands for a decade, with Guillaume Peretti taking it in 2014, then D’Haene holding it from 2016 to 2021, and Labert Santelli beating the time by 40 minutes. On July 9, 2026, D’Haene reclaimed the FKT, his third time completing the route. And now, Dunand-Pallaz has etched his name into GR20 history.

Multi-Year Preparation

According to Dunand-Pallaz, he has a deep connection to the island that stretches back to when he was a child and would visit with his parents. Thoughts about an FKT effort on the trail first began to take shape in 2020, and he notes that the trail holds a mythical status for many French runners.

For Dunand-Pallaz, the steep and technical terrain suited his running style. He’d done five reconnaissance runs on the trail over the past six years, including one a month before the attempt, to ensure he knew it as well as possible, especially the more technical northern part.

For his crew, Dunand-Pallaz followed the Corsican Mountain Federation guidelines, which allowed a maximum of two pacers at a time who could carry his nutrition and other gear. He also made sure to have his GR20 notebook signed by each hut keeper along the route.

All-Day Record Pace

After setting off, it didn’t take long for Dunand-Pallaz to start tracking ahead of record pace. In just 23k, he was almost 29 minutes up on D’Haene’s splits. By the time he reached the Col de Verde, about 145k into the effort, he was tracking nearly two hours ahead of record pace. Dunand-Pallaz reached the Col de Bavella, with 19k to go — with 700 meters of climbing and 1,670 meters of descending left — he’d built his buffer on the record to nearly 2.5 hours.

While the pacing may seem calculated, according to Dunand-Pallaz, he ran the route by feel, only checking his splits after 40k, and again at 75k. Though he knew he had a strong lead, he continued to push the entire way and called the effort one of his best performances ever.

Aurélien Dunand-Pallaz climbs a rocky section of the GR20. Photo: @rising.story

Dunand-Pallaz was assisted by a team of 10 pacers — and also close friends — that included Théo Detienne, Arthur Joyeux-Bouillon, Diego Pazos, Julien Michelon, and Daniel Antonioli.

While this appears to be Dunand-Pallaz’s first recognized FKT, which is still awaiting verification by the Fastest Known Time website, he’s no stranger to 100-mile efforts in the mountains. In 2023, he won both the Hardrock 100 and Diagonale des Fous, and in 2021, he placed second at UTMB and won the mountainous Transgrancanaria. Earlier this year, Dunand-Pallaz placed fourth at the Madeira Island Ultra-Trail in April and won the Restonica Trail 110k in July.

When asked to compare the GR20 to his other mountain races, Dunand-Pallaz thinks that the GR20 was much harder due to its relentless nature. Though he has no other FKT efforts planned in the immediate future, Dunand-Pallaz says he’s planning to put in for the 2027 Hardrock 100 lottery.
